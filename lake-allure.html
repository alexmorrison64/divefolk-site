Where to dive near DC · Pennsylvania quarry

Lake Allure, Pennsylvania

A small, private quarry lake in Lancaster County farm country, open to divers on weekends. Only 30 divers fit at once, so it's quiet, and it's about two and a quarter hours from DC.

At a glance

  • Where: 140 North Church Street, Quarryville, PA 17566. Look for the dive flag by the road and turn in beside the old stone house.
  • From DC: about 110 miles, roughly 2¼ hours by car from the White House. Traffic on I-95 can add a lot.
  • Open: Saturdays and Sundays only. Gates open at 8 AM, and divers must be packed up by 4 PM.
  • Season: the 2026 season opened April 11. Dive Peer pauses its monthly passes from December through March, so plan on spring to late fall and check before you drive.
  • Run by: Dive Peer, which also runs Willow Springs in Lebanon County, PA. divepeer.com, 717-693-2440, DivePeer@gmail.com
  • Room for: 30 divers at the lake at once.

Tickets

Buy online on Dive Peer's tickets page. Prices change, so check there.

  • Diver day ticket: $65
  • Non-diver ("Bubble Watcher") day ticket: $20
  • 3-month pass for both Lake Allure and Willow Springs: $195, the same as three day tickets
  • Monthly pass for both lakes: $65 a month, two months minimum

Everyone also signs Dive Peer's waiver, dated for the day you dive. Blank copies are at the lake.

What to expect

You park at the top, then a staff Polaris carries your gear down the hill to the lake, back and forth all day. No cars on the hill, no exceptions. At the water there's a floating dock, boat-style benches with numbered seats and cubbies for your gear, and a porta-potty. There's no running water on the property, so bring what you need to drink and rinse.

Straight out from the dock, bearing a little right, are three training platforms side by side along a retaining wall. Classes use them a lot, so give students room.

Millersville University students mapped the lake's bottom with sonar and an underwater robot in 2021, as it was being turned into a dive training site.

What divers say about the water

Dive Peer doesn't publish depth, visibility or temperature, so this part comes from divers' reviews, not the lake. Treat the numbers as rough.

  • Depth: reviewers put the deepest point anywhere from about 70 to 90 feet. Much of the training area is shallower sand.
  • Visibility: one DiveBuddy listing says it averages 16 to 20 feet. An instructor who taught Open Water there in August 2023 reported 25 to 30 feet and called the water crystal clear.
  • Temperature: DiveBuddy reviewers list the upper 60s°F in summer and the upper 50s in spring and fall. One diver's video says it gets cold below about 40 feet, so plan for a thermocline.
  • Things to see: a DiveBuddy reviewer mentions a sunk Cadillac Escalade and a sailboat. Dive site listings mention bass, catfish, perch, bluegill, sunfish, crappie and freshwater snails.
  • Safety: a DiveBuddy reviewer says the site is staffed during open hours and has an oxygen kit.

Rules worth knowing before you go

  • Show your certification card. The only exception is students in an entry-level class with their instructor.
  • Required gear for every diver: a BCD, a pressure gauge, a depth gauge and timer or a dive computer, and an alternate air source.
  • No solo diving, ever, whatever your certification. Pass holders caught diving solo lose their pass.
  • No swimming, snorkeling, wading or kayaking unless you're in a class.
  • No spearguns, no fishing, no alcohol, no camping, no grills, and no jumping from the walls.
  • No pets. Service animals and emotional support animals with a letter are welcome, on land only.
  • Children under 12 need an adult with them at all times.
  • Scooters only for divers certified to use them, or in a class.

Full list: Lake Allure rules and regulations.

Good to know for newer divers

  • Bring filled tanks. We couldn't find air fills or rental gear at the lake on Dive Peer's pages. Fill up at your shop before you leave, or call Dive Peer to ask.
  • Bring lunch, or go into town. There are several restaurants within a mile. Picnic tables at the lake are for instructors.
  • Small is a plus. A small, quiet lake with platforms is a good place to practice buoyancy and work on new skills without a crowd.
  • Plan your exposure suit for the cold layer, not the warm surface. See our wetsuits and drysuits guide.
  • Print our free shore dive emergency plan and fill it in for Lake Allure.
