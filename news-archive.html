Week of November 23, 2026

Nov 20, 2026 · Dive tech update

Garmin Descent G1 update improves cold-water buttons and fixes random dive locations

Software 17.25 for the Descent G1 series, live for all users since Nov 20, adds better in-dive alerts for unexpected power problems and makes the touch-sensitive buttons respond better in cold water. It also fixes dive entry and exit points that could land in random spots (often near Africa) and stops the flashlight shortcut from working during dives.

Source: Garmin

Nov 16, 2026 · Training and safety

US regulators warn snorkellers to stop using some Qing Song full-face masks

The CPSC says certain discontinued Qing Song masks, recognizable by a shark-fin shaped snorkel tube and sold on Amazon and TikTok from January 2019 to August 2026, can build up carbon dioxide and lead to breathing trouble or drowning. Owners should stop using them and throw them away rather than sell or give them away; the maker has not agreed to a recall.

Source: Divernet

Nov 21, 2026 · Training and safety

Cyprus weighs licensing rules for dive centres

A bill now before the Cypriot parliament would require licensed dive providers, properly qualified instructors, valid insurance and compliance with the ISO 24803 standard, which only 8 to 10 of the island's roughly 80 operators currently meet. The proposal follows three recreational diving deaths in Cyprus this year and is still at an early stage.

Source: Divernet

Nov 17, 2026 · Industry

PADI gets new leaders as Drew Richardson steps back

After 24 years holding both top jobs, Drew Richardson is handing over day-to-day control, with Chad Kuehn becoming CEO and Kristin Valette Wirth becoming president from the start of 2027. Kuehn says a digital and marketing overhaul comes first, while Valette Wirth will run regional operations and keep oversight of PADI's conservation work.

Source: Divernet

Nov 20, 2026 · Science

Wreck of Australia's first home-built warship found off Queensland

Navy clearance divers and maritime archaeologists found HMCS Spitfire, launched in Sydney in 1855 and lost in a gale in 1898, off Far North Queensland on Aug 21, exactly 128 years after it sank. The wreck is protected by law, no public diving access has been announced, and archaeologists hope to return in 2027.

Source: The Scuba News

Nov 20, 2026 · Science

Fossil reefs in Jamaica point to where Caribbean corals may survive

University of Texas researchers compared 125,000-year-old fossil reefs with living ones and found that the big branching Acropora corals that once dominated are now mostly dead and covered in algae. Using past conditions and climate models, they flag parts of the northern Caribbean, the Florida Keys and the Yucatan as the best bets for future reef conservation.

Source: UT News

Week of November 16, 2026

Nov 6, 2026 · Dive tech update

Garmin Descent X30 update cuts nuisance ascent alarms and fixes a dive log freeze

Software 5.02 for the Descent X30 now sounds the ascent rate alarm only after a sustained fast ascent, cutting false alerts, and adds a page explaining what Setup Sharing transfers. It also fixes a freeze when the log holds more than 255 dives, random dive entry and exit locations, and an upside-down compass after booting face-down.

Source: Garmin

Nov 12, 2026 · Travel

SS United States is now on the bottom off Destin as the world's largest artificial reef

After a weather-driven move to Nov 11 and hours of slow flooding, the 302m liner sank about 22 nautical miles southwest of Destin in roughly 55m of water. The plan was for it to sit upright with the top of the superstructure near 18m, within reach of open water divers, though that was not yet confirmed at press time.

Source: Divernet

Nov 15, 2026 · Science

Divers map a 1.5km coral formation off Australia's Cocos Keeling Islands

A team led by Adelaide University's JP Hobbs took 12 dives to map a continuous stretch of Pachyseris rose coral running 1.56km along a remote reef, from 14 to 65m deep. Genetic tests on about 50 samples will show whether it is one giant colony, which could be hundreds or even thousands of years old.

Source: Divernet

Nov 12, 2026 · Travel

Madeira's dive fees jump to €12.50 a day from April 2027

Divers in Madeira's marine reserves, such as Garajau and the Desertas, and on its artificial reef wrecks will pay €12.50 per person per day, up from €7.50, or €8.50 through dive centres with an IFCN agreement. Residents and children 12 and under are exempt, so ask your dive centre whether the fee is already in the price.

Source: The Scuba News

Nov 9, 2026 · Travel

Four Cozumel dive sites stay closed through Nov 30

San Francisco, San Francisco Shallows, Santa Rosa and Santa Rosa Wall are closed to diving and snorkelling under the marine park's rotating reef rest program. Palancar Caves, Palancar Bricks and the Colombia sites have reopened, so check your planned sites with your operator before you go.

Source: The Scuba News

Nov 12, 2026 · Ocean

Blue Friday asks divers to dive, not shop, on Nov 27

Started by a California dive club in 2021, Blue Friday encourages divers to spend the day after US Thanksgiving diving, snorkelling or doing a beach clean-up instead of shopping. Clubs and dive centres from Roatan to the Great Barrier Reef are taking part this year.

Source: Divernet

Week of November 9, 2026

Nov 3, 2026 · Gear

Scubapro launches the Aladin 100, a simple first dive computer

The Aladin 100 has a large backlit screen, two buttons, nitrox support up to 50 percent and a user-replaceable AA battery rated for over 300 dive hours. It sends logs by Bluetooth to LogTRAK 2.0 and sells for about £219 in the UK.

Source: Divernet

Nov 6, 2026 · Training and safety

UK incident report flags rising decompression sickness and drysuit inversions

BSAC's yearly report counted nine UK diving deaths in 2025, down from 12, but decompression illness cases rose to 53, with repeat diving a growing factor. It urges divers to get familiar with new or borrowed drysuits and boots and to practise inversion recovery early in the season.

Source: The Scuba News

Nov 7, 2026 · Travel

Judge declines to stop SS United States reef sinking as new challenge is filed

A federal judge in Florida denied a preservation group's emergency request to halt the sinking, and the group has filed an amended motion. Okaloosa County says the ship is still set to go down off Destin on Nov 12, weather permitting.

Source: The Scuba News

Nov 5, 2026 · Travel

Malaysia's new dive guide licensing rules near enforcement

Malaysia now requires dive guides to be licensed citizens with at least divemaster training, working for a licensed dive centre, with active enforcement expected after April 15, 2027. Many operators were not yet licensed as of September, so visiting divers may want to check before booking.

Source: The Scuba News

Oct 31, 2026 · Science

Coral bacteria make hormones that help corals survive heat

Researchers at NYU Abu Dhabi found that Endozoicomonas bacteria living with Arabian Gulf corals produce hormones as water warms, helping coral cells cope when heat disrupts the coral's own hormone output. The team says such microbes could one day support reef restoration.

Source: Eurasia Review

The yearly CCAMLR meeting in Hobart closed without agreeing to any proposed Antarctic marine protected areas or new limits on krill fishing. No new protected area has been adopted there in about a decade.

Source: Antarctic and Southern Ocean Coalition

Stanford researchers placed bleached coral fragments against healthy fragments from the same colony. The tissues joined and passed along the algae corals need, and the bleached pieces recovered in about seven weeks.
