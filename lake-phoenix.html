Where to dive near DC · Virginia quarry

Lake Phoenix, Virginia

A former rock quarry in Rawlings, Virginia, run as a scuba park and campground. It's where many DC-area divers do their Open Water dives, and it's open all year.

At a glance

  • Where: 1 Quarry Lane, Rawlings, VA 23876, just off I-85 south of Petersburg.
  • From DC: about 165 miles, roughly 3 hours by car from the White House. Traffic on I-95 can add a lot.
  • Open: all year. Regular diving days are Thursday to Monday, plus Tuesday and Wednesday in summer. Winter hours are shorter, and some days need a reservation, so check the park's calendar before you drive.
  • Shop hours: Mon to Thu 9 to 5, Fri 9 to 6, Sat 8 to 6, Sun 8 to 5. The lake closes one hour before the shop.
  • Depth: about 60 feet at the deepest. The park doesn't publish a maximum depth.
  • Who can dive: certified divers and divers in training, plus freedivers. Bring your certification card or driver's license. No children under 5 in the park, and no pets.
  • Contact: (804) 716-2199, lakephoenixva@gmail.com, lakephoenixva.com

Water temperature and the thermocline

In summer the top of the lake can be over 80°F, but deeper down you hit a thermocline, a sudden cold layer. The park says it sits around 44 to 58°F, so plan your wetsuit for the cold water, not the warm surface. In winter the water can drop below 50°F.

The park posts current temperatures by depth on its home page. On November 18, 2026 it read about 63°F from the surface to 50 feet, with no thermocline.

What's underwater

The park has sunk a plane, a helicopter, vans, school buses and boats, plus training platforms. A large dive map by the docks shows where everything is; take a photo of it before your first dive. Look for bluegill, bass, turtles and crayfish.

Prices

Per person, per day. Prices change, so check the park's pricing page.

  • Diver admission, Thursday to Monday: $34.75
  • Diver admission with a tank and unlimited air: $57.25
  • Diver admission, Tuesday or Wednesday in summer: $31.25
  • Freediver: $15.00
  • Visitor or snorkeler with a dive group: $8.50
  • Complete rental package (admission, full gear, unlimited air): $145.75
  • Single air fill: $10.50; nitrox fill: $22.75
  • 10-admission punch card, which never expires: $312.75

Rentals, air and nitrox

  • Full gear rental on site, including 3mm and 7mm wetsuits, hoods, gloves, lights and compasses. Rental hours are shorter than shop hours, and late returns cost an extra day.
  • Air and nitrox fills on site. Nitrox tanks must be labeled for oxygen service, and the shop won't fill some older aluminum tanks.
  • Bring your own tank and you can fill it during the day; same-day refills must be in before 4 PM.

Camping and places to stay

You can stay right at the lake: tent sites (with or without power), RV sites, cottages and rental RVs, plus a bath house and covered pavilions for gearing up. A "Camper Special" covers two diving days, two tent nights and unlimited air. Camping fees don't include admission. See the park's pricing page for current rates and the reservations page to book.

Good to know before you go

  • Fill out the park's waiver online before you arrive.
  • Local dive clubs say first-time visitors each year watch a short orientation video. We couldn't confirm this on the park's own site.
  • Fishing isn't allowed, and only electric golf carts and solar generators are allowed.
  • Bring lunch and drinks. There's a small shop, but don't count on much food.
  • Many DC-area dive shops run group days here, which is an easy way to find a buddy.
  • Print our free shore dive emergency plan and fill it in for Lake Phoenix before your first visit.

From Alex, who started DiveFolk

I did my Open Water dives here with Aquatic Adventures in 2025, then navigation, night and wreck dives that fall. What I learned:

  • Learn the map. This is the big one. The bus, boats, helicopter and platforms make great landmarks once you know the layout, and I used them for compass practice at night too. Study the big map by the docks before you get in.
  • The cold layer is real. On a September day the surface was 83°F, but below about 38 feet it was cold enough that I turned back from the helicopter.
  • Visibility changes a lot. I had about 30 feet in the fall, and closer to 10 feet on a late-May afternoon, with lots of silt and pollen.
  • A steel tank changes your weighting. With a steel 119 I went from 11 lb, which was too heavy, to 6 lb. Do a weight check first.
