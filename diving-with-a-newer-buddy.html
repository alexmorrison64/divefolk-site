New to diving · Buddies

Diving with a less experienced buddy

One of the best things about diving is that new and experienced divers can enjoy the same dive. It just takes a plan built around the newer diver.

The rule that covers most of it

Plan the dive around the less experienced diver: their training, their comfort, their air use. DAN advises buddies to set the pace, depth and time together and to plan for the slower or less experienced diver.

Before you get in

  • Talk honestly. How many dives, how recently, where, and anything that makes them nervous. A diver with 100 dives ten years ago may be rusty.
  • Agree on the plan: maximum depth (within the newer diver's training), how long, the route, and who leads.
  • Set a turn pressure and an end pressure. Start back when either of you reaches the turn pressure, and plan to surface with a reserve, commonly 500 psi.
  • Review hand signals, including "something's wrong," "low on air" and "end the dive."
  • Agree on a lost-buddy plan. A common one: look for about a minute, then go up slowly and meet at the surface.
  • Do a full buddy check and show each other where your alternate air source and weight releases are.

In the water

  • Stay close, within easy reach. Check on each other often, not just air.
  • The newer diver often uses air faster. Ask for their pressure more often than you think you need to.
  • Keep it simple. A new site, cold water, low visibility and a deep plan all at once is too much for one dive.
  • Anyone can call any dive, for any reason. When one buddy ends the dive, it's over for both.

If you're the newer diver

  • Say what you're comfortable with. A good buddy will plan around it.
  • Don't follow anyone past your training or your comfort.
  • Ending a dive early is never wrong.

Want more practice? A Rescue Diver course is one of the best ways to become a better buddy.
