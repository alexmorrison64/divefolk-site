New to diving · Gear

Buying dive gear and accessories without wasting money

Some gear is worth every dollar. Some you can buy cheap. Here's how to tell the difference.

Start at your dive shop, and ask about price matching

Shop prices can look high next to big online stores, but many dive shops will match the price of well-known online sellers. Ask. You get the same price plus a fitting, someone to call when something goes wrong, and service down the road. That matters most for anything you breathe from.

Never go cheap on life-support gear

  • Regulator, BCD, dive computer and gauges: buy from a dive shop or authorized dealer, from makers with service networks. Have them serviced on the maker's schedule.
  • Drysuit: fit and quality matter too much to save money here. See our wetsuit and drysuit guide.
  • Never buy a used regulator unless it's serviced by a qualified technician before you dive it.

Where a budget option can be fine

  • 3mm and 5mm wetsuits, if you don't dive often. Wetsuits compress and wear out over time anyway. A lower-priced suit from a big online store can be good value, as long as it fits snugly with no gaps.
  • Stainless steel clips and double-ender bolt snaps. Cheap ones are often fine if they're solid stainless steel; marine-grade 316 holds up best. Avoid plated or "alloy" clips that rust, and very small clips that are hard to work with gloves on.
  • Spares for your save-a-dive kit: mask and fin straps, O-rings, zip ties, mouthpieces.

Where cheap lets you down: retractors

Gear retractors keep things like a light, camera or compass close to your body. Cheap ones often can't hold the weight they claim, so heavier items end up dangling, dragging or getting lost. For anything heavy, buy a heavy-duty retractor from a dive brand at your dive shop, with stainless steel hardware and a retraction force that's actually rated for your gear.

From Alex, who started DiveFolk

  • I don't dive often enough to justify an expensive 3mm or 5mm wetsuit, so lower-cost wetsuits from Amazon have worked well for me.
  • Stainless clips and double-enders from Temu have been fine.
  • Cheap retractors were the exception. They didn't hold my heavier gear, even though they said they would, so I now buy heavy-duty retractors at the dive shop.
  • My shop, Aquatic Adventures, price matches reasonable online sites, so I always ask.
