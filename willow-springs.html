Where to dive near DC · Pennsylvania quarry

Willow Springs, Pennsylvania

A spring-fed old quarry in Lebanon County that has been a dive training lake since the 1970s. It's packed with sunk attractions, including a 72-foot trawler and, since May 2026, a Learjet. It's about three hours from DC.

A note from DiveFolk: we haven't confirmed how many DC-area divers go to Willow Springs. It's mostly used by Pennsylvania dive shops for training. If you've dived it from the DC area, use the tip box below and tell us.

At a glance

  • Where: 199 Millardsville Road, Richland, PA 17087, in Jackson Township, Lebanon County.
  • From DC: about 165 miles, roughly 3 hours by car from the White House. That's farther than Dive Peer's other lake, Lake Allure.
  • Open: Saturdays and Sundays, 8 AM to 4 PM. The gate opens at 8 and no one gets in before then.
  • Season: the 2026 season opened April 11. Dive Peer pauses its monthly passes from December through March, so plan on spring to late fall and check before you drive.
  • Run by: Dive Peer, the family that runs Lancaster Scuba. divepeer.com, 717-693-2440, DivePeer@gmail.com
  • The lake: spring-fed, with a maximum depth of about 55 feet. It was a working quarry until the early 1900s.

Tickets

Buy online on Dive Peer's tickets page. The passes work at both Willow Springs and Lake Allure.

  • Diver day ticket: $65
  • Non-diver ("Bubble Watcher") day ticket: $20
  • 3-month pass for both lakes: $195
  • Monthly pass for both lakes: $65 a month, two months minimum

Everyone signs Dive Peer's waiver, dated for the day you dive, and checks in with staff before getting in the water. Bring your certification card.

What's underwater

This is the fun part. Willow Springs has been collecting sunk attractions for decades.

  • A Learjet, sunk on May 14, 2026. It's a retired 44-foot jet that spent about 20 years flying donor organs. It hangs in the water instead of sitting on the bottom, so you can swim around and inside it.
  • The Quest, a 72-foot wooden fishing trawler sunk in 1977. A dive club wrote in 2019 that it sits in about 42 feet of water and its mast reaches the surface, which makes it easy to find.
  • And more: a fire truck, a caboose, a steam shovel, a torpedo, a school bus, a cement truck and what's left of a Volkswagen.

What divers say about the water

Dive Peer doesn't publish visibility or temperature, so these come from divers and the news, not the lake.

  • Visibility: reviewers usually say 10 to 20 feet. A Google reviewer reported about 15 feet in August 2023.
  • Temperature: a dive club measured about 75°F at the surface and about 55°F at 45 feet in July 2019. Dress for the cold layer.
  • Algae: in summer 2023 an algae bloom closed the lake for three weeks. The operators then spent $80,000 on a treatment to clear the water.
  • Navigation: a DiveBuddy reviewer liked the well-labeled lines running between the attractions.

Facilities

  • Air fills and tank rentals at the office, plus a small store with accessories (not full gear).
  • Three porta-potties. No running water, so bring what you need to drink and rinse.
  • You can set up a tent and table on the grass by the lake. Gas grills and camp stoves are fine; ground fires are not.
  • Parking is limited, so carpool if you can. No parking on the grass.

Rules worth knowing before you go

  • Show your certification card. The only exception is students in an entry-level class with their instructor.
  • Required gear for every diver: a BCD, a pressure gauge, a depth gauge and timer or a dive computer, and an alternate air source.
  • No solo diving, ever, whatever your certification. Pass holders caught diving solo lose their pass.
  • No swimming, snorkeling, wading or kayaking unless you're in a class.
  • No spearguns, no fishing, no alcohol, and no jumping from the walls.
  • Children under 12 need an adult with them at all times.
  • Don't touch, move or take anything from the sunk attractions.

Full list: Willow Springs rules and regulations.

Good to know for newer divers

  • Expect classes. The lake is mostly used for training, so weekends can be busy with students. Arrive near 8 AM and expect a short wait while staff park everyone.
  • Not too deep and full of things to see is a good mix for practicing navigation, as long as you stay off the attractions.
  • Going inside the Learjet is an overhead. If you haven't been trained for that, look from the outside.
  • Print our free shore dive emergency plan and fill it in for Willow Springs.
