Steel vs aluminum tanks, and your weighting

Switch tanks and your weighting changes. Here's why, and how to get it right without guessing.

The short version

  • Steel tanks sink, full or empty.
  • Aluminum tanks, like the common aluminum 80, start a little heavy and float by the end of the dive.
  • So with a steel tank you usually need less lead on your belt or in your pockets.

DAN puts it simply: steel tanks tend to sink whether full or empty, while aluminum tanks often start negative and turn positive as you use the air, so divers using steel need less lead.

Why it happens

Air has weight. An 80 cubic foot tank holds roughly 6 pounds of air, so every tank gets about that much lighter as you breathe it down. What differs is where each tank starts:

  • A typical aluminum 80 is about 1.5 pounds heavy when full and about 3 to 4 pounds floaty when nearly empty.
  • A typical steel tank is several pounds heavy when full and still a few pounds heavy when nearly empty.

That floaty aluminum tank at the end of your dive is exactly when you need to hold your safety stop. Your lead has to cover it.

Exact numbers vary by maker and model; check the spec sheet for your tank.

What it meant for one diver

DiveFolk's founder dives at Lake Phoenix in a 3mm wetsuit. With a steel 119 tank, 11 pounds of lead turned out to be too much, and 6 pounds was right. Your number will be different, which is why the weight check matters.

Do a weight check whenever something changes

New tank, new wetsuit, fresh water to salt water, or a long break: check your weighting on the first dive. A common way, taught in most courses:

  • At the end of a dive, with about 500 psi left, stay at the surface with your BCD empty.
  • Hold a normal breath. You should float with the water around eye level.
  • When you breathe out, you should start to sink slowly.
  • Too floaty? Add a pound or two. Sinking with a breath held? Take some off. Write the answer in your logbook with the tank and suit you used.

Ask a dive professional to help the first time. Being overweighted is common and makes every dive harder.
