Technology Explained · Dive log apps

Which dive log app?

Short answer: for most divers, try Submersion (free, phone and computer) or Subsurface (free, desktop). Here's how the main free apps compare, so you can pick one and stick with it, and where the DiveFolk Logbook fits.

The short answer

  • Want a full logbook on your phone and your computer? Try Submersion.
  • Want a desktop program that has been around for years? Try Subsurface.
  • Want your log on a website you can open anywhere? Try divelogs.org.
  • Only dive one brand of computer and happy with its app? Shearwater Cloud or Garmin Dive is the easiest place to start.
  • Need to fix split dives or match a paper logbook first? Use the DiveFolk Logbook, then take the clean file to any of these.

All of these are free. DiveFolk has no deals with any of them and no affiliate links.

Submersion

A free, open-source dive log for iPhone, Android, Mac, Windows and Linux. No ads, no subscription and no in-app purchases. Your log is kept on your own device, and you can sync between devices through your own iCloud, Google Drive or Dropbox.

  • Downloads from more than 350 dive computers by Bluetooth or USB.
  • Imports logs from Subsurface, Shearwater Cloud, Garmin, DL7, UDDF and CSV, and can read paper logbook pages with your phone's camera.
  • Exports your whole log to UDDF or CSV at any time.

Good to know: it went public in March 2026 and changes quickly, so expect new features and the odd bug. It has a lot for technical and rebreather divers, which can make it feel busy if you're new.

Sources: Submersion on GitHub and its ScubaBoard thread.

Subsurface

A free, open-source dive log program for Windows, Mac and Linux, first released in 2011, with a mobile app for iPhone and Android. Free cloud storage is optional.

  • Downloads from a wide range of dive computers.
  • Can merge dives your computer split in two.
  • Imports UDDF, DL7, CSV and logs from many other programs.

Good to know: many divers find its screens old-fashioned, and the Android app isn't in the Google Play store; you download it from Subsurface's website.

Sources: subsurface-divelog.org and its FAQ.

divelogs.org

A free dive log on the web, running since 2004. It says it has no ads and doesn't sell your data. It imports files from many dive computers and apps, syncs with Subsurface and Diving Log, and has a companion app for iPhone and iPad.

Good to know: you need an account, and your log lives on their website. Check its export options so you keep your own copy too.

Source: divelogs' own ScubaBoard post.

The maker apps: Shearwater Cloud and Garmin Dive

Shearwater Cloud

Free from Shearwater, on phones and computers. It downloads your Shearwater dives and can export them as UDDF or CSV. It can't merge dives that were split in two, and users report you can't add a dive by hand.

Sources: Shearwater Cloud; Shearwater support, October 2026.

Garmin Dive

Free with Garmin Descent computers and other Garmin devices that dive. It logs scuba and freediving, with maps, dive sites, gear and certifications, and syncs to Garmin Connect. Divers on Garmin's forums say you can't bring in dives from other apps, and we haven't found a way to merge split dives. You can download your dives as FIT files from Garmin Connect on a computer.

Sources: Garmin Dive in the App Store; Garmin forums.

Good to know: maker apps are easiest when you stay with one brand. If you switch brands later, export your dives first so you can take them with you.

Where the DiveFolk Logbook fits

The DiveFolk Logbook isn't a logbook app. It's a free tool that runs in your browser with nothing to install, for cleaning up your dives before you keep or move them:

  • Joins dives your computer split in two.
  • Fixes dives logged in the wrong time zone.
  • Matches your paper logbook to your computer dives and flags anything that doesn't agree.
  • Downloads a spreadsheet (CSV) or a DL7 file that Submersion and Subsurface can import.

If you're starting fresh with Submersion or Subsurface, they can read your dive computer directly, and you may not need DiveFolk at all. DiveFolk helps most when your dives are split, your times are off, or your best notes are on paper.

One thing none of them do

PADI's online logbook doesn't accept dive files, so dives logged with PADI still have to be typed in one at a time. A clean spreadsheet with every dive on one row makes that faster.
