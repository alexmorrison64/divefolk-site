Where to dive near DC · Road trip

North Carolina wrecks

The waters off Morehead City and Beaufort are called the Graveyard of the Atlantic. Warm Gulf Stream water, World War II wrecks, and sand tiger sharks that hang around the wrecks. It's the closest big-ocean diving to DC.

Is it for you yet?

Most of the famous wrecks are deep, around 90 to 130 feet, and well offshore. That's deeper than Open Water training covers, and charter operators set requirements before they'll take you. One shop's listing for an offshore Olympus charter asks for:

  • Advanced Open Water (or Deep) and nitrox certifications
  • At least 25 logged dives
  • At least 2 ocean dives to 70 feet or deeper, and 2 boat dives
  • Age 15 or older

Newer divers aren't shut out. Shallower inshore wrecks around 50 to 70 feet are a good first trip, and some shops run Advanced or Deep courses on these charters. Ask the operator what you qualify for when you book.

The wrecks people ask for

  • U-352: a German submarine sunk in 1942, in about 115 feet of water.
  • Papoose: a tanker lying upside down in about 120 feet, known for sand tiger sharks.
  • Spar: a Coast Guard cutter in about 110 feet, a favorite for sand tigers and often the second dive after the U-352.
  • Aeolus, Caribsea, Atlas and Schurz: other popular offshore wrecks.
  • Shallower wrecks such as the Indra, Titan, Suloide and Hutton sit around 50 to 70 feet.

Captains pick the sites the morning of the trip, based on weather and seas.

Sand tiger sharks

Sand tigers look fierce, with rows of teeth showing, but they're calm around divers. They aren't fed; you see them living around the wrecks. Give them space and enjoy it. You can send profile photos of them to the Spot A Shark USA citizen science project.

Conditions and season

  • Summer water is usually in the 70s to about 80°F offshore, with visibility often 60 feet or more. Many divers still wear a 5mm wetsuit.
  • Conditions change fast. Visibility can differ a lot between two nearby wrecks, and wind and swells cancel trips. Plan extra days.
  • Boats leave early, often around 6 AM, and the run offshore can take two hours.
  • Charters dive year-round. Summer is busiest; book early.

How the diving works

  • Boats usually drop you on the wreck with a briefing and let you and your buddy dive your own plan. There often isn't a guide in the water, so you need to be comfortable on your own.
  • Bring your own dive computer and a surface marker buoy (SMB); some charters require both.
  • Nitrox is common, and some offshore charters require it.
  • Save DAN's Emergency Hotline in your phone: +1-919-684-9111. DAN says to call local emergency services first, then DAN.

Getting there and where to stay

Morehead City is about 370 miles from DC, roughly 6 hours by car from the White House, and Beaufort is just across the bridge. Operators include Olympus Dive Center (713 Shepard Street, Morehead City, 252-726-9432), which has a simple bunkhouse lodge near its dock, and Discovery Diving in Beaufort. Many DC-area dive shops run group trips here, which is the easiest way to go the first time.
