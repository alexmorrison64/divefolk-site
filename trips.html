Trip report · December 2025

Dominica: easy boat dives, lots of life, and a lot to do on land

By Alex, DiveFolk's founder. 14 dives over five days, from a new diver's point of view.

Where
Dominica, Caribbean. Stayed at the Fort Young Hotel in Roseau, which has its own dive shop.
When
December 7 to 11, 2025
Organized by
A group trip run by Aquatic Adventures, a dive shop in Virginia
Diving
Morning boat dives on the west coast, plus night and training dives from the hotel's dock
Conditions
Water 79 to 82 °F. Visibility 60 to 100 feet on boat dives. Mostly light current. Most dives 40 to 90 feet.
Exposure
A 3 mm wetsuit on day one, then just a rash guard and boots
Recommend it?
Yes.
Best for
New divers, and anyone who wants plenty to do between dives

The diving

The sites I dove included Champagne Reef, Scotts Head Drop Off, Soufrière Pinnacle, Solomon, Witches Point, Dangleben's Pinnacle and a wall dive. Champagne Reef is the best known: volcanic gas bubbles stream up from the sea floor.

Some favorite moments from my log: a turtle in a small canyon between pinnacles on the first dive, parrotfish everywhere at Champagne South, and a pufferfish with blue, glittery eyes. On a night dive off the hotel dock we saw bioluminescence on the way out, and back at the dock, moray eels were cleaning up lionfish scraps.

An honest note: most of the coral I saw was dead. But the sponges were great and the dives were full of life, with schooling fish and lots of small fish along the ledges and slopes. Mostly calm water and good visibility made it a relaxed place to build experience, which is why I think it's a great spot for new divers. We really liked our boat captain and dive masters, too.

Out of the water

I loved the dives, but I especially loved walking around Roseau and eating where the locals eat, including neighborhoods people told me to avoid. I never had a problem. Use the same common sense you would in any city, and get out of the hotel.

Don't skip the side trips. Dominica is mountains and rainforest, and the hikes to waterfalls and river gorges were some of the best days of the trip. Plan them for afternoons or a day off from diving.

Good to know

  • Pack boots and a thin wetsuit or rash guard; the water is warm.
  • Bring a dive light if you want to try a night dive off the dock.
  • Dock dives at Fort Young depend on the cruise ship schedule. When a ship stays late, night dives can't start until it leaves, so plan for some waiting.
  • Cost and flights: not covered here yet. We'll add them when more divers share reports.
